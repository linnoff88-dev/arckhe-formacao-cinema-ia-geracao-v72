Lesson 6 · 17 min

Several shots and lines in one generation: multishot and dialogue

In 2026, some generators make several shots with cuts in a single generation, keeping the character and light, and they already put the dialogue in the speaker's mouth. This saves you from stitching clips together, but it asks for a more precise script: numbered shots, who says what, and how long each one lasts.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What multishot is and which generators do it as of Oct 2026
  2. 2Writing numbered shots inside a single generation
  3. 3Dialogue with who says what, in short lines
  4. 4When it's better to generate shot by shot

Goal: write a multishot script of up to 15 s with numbered shots, who says what, and the sound of each shot.

02 · Writing the multishot

The example is the end of "The first batch": the door, the line, and the first customer.

1. Who does it as of Oct 2026

Runway Gen-4.5: several shots and native audio, videos up to 1 min (Dec 2025). Kling 3.0 Omni: per-shot control of duration, angle, and movement. LTX-2.5, open: native multishot, according to the official page.

Kling with 6 cuts or 30 s per generation shows up only on third-party sites: treat it as reported and check it in the tool.

SourcesTechCrunch, 12/11/2025 (Runway); Adobe blog, 04/15/2026 (Kling Omni); Hugging Face, LTX-2.5 (checked on 10/19/2026).

2. Shots inside the generation

Write it like a short shot list: number, size, one movement, duration, and action. The numbering tells the generator where to cut.

Keep the lock and the look the same across all shots: inside a single generation the generator tends to hold continuity, but it can't guess what you didn't write.

3. Dialogue

Kling 3.0 launched with dialogue between multiple characters in multiple languages. For any generator, write who is speaking and the text, like in Google's guide: "The baker says: Coming!".

One line of dialogue per shot, and short sentences. A long line doesn't fit in 3 seconds, and the generator speeds it up or cuts it off.

Shot 4sound: door bell; the customer says: The usual, Dona Lia.

4. Multishot or shot by shot

Multishot works for short, continuous stretches with the same light and the same people, like a conversation.

Shot by shot (lesson 4) works when each shot needs to be redone on its own, or when the generator you chose doesn't do multishot.

03 · Templates to copy

The multishot script that the page checks.

multishot.txt (template)
Scene: <name> (<total duration> s, one generation)
Generator: <name and version (Oct/2026)>, limit of <n> s
Characters: <who appears, fictional>
<n> | <shot size> | <one movement> | <n> s | <what happens> | sound: <ambience and effect>; <who> says: <short text>
<n> | <shot size> | <one movement> | <n> s | <what happens> | sound: <ambience and effect>

06 · Practice

A short stretch of your scene, preferably with a line of dialogue.

Script · one stretch in one generation

20 min

Pick 2 to 6 consecutive shots from your shot list that happen in the same place and with the same light. Write the multishot.txt with at least one line of dialogue in the "Name says: text" format, adding up to between 6 and 15 s. Paste it into "Check your multishot script" and download the file.

Done when: the page answers OK: [n] shots in one generation · [s] s and the multishot.txt is in the project folder.

Optional · generate and check the clip

30 min

If your generator does multishot, generate the stretch in 16:9 with the script and the base prompt from lesson 3. Check the file in the lesson 4 clip checker.

Done when: the lesson 4 checker answers OK: [s] s · [w]×[h] · 16:9 for the clip and it is in the project folder next to the multishot.txt.
